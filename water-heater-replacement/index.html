Signs the tank is finished

Rust-colored hot water, a puddle that returns after you wipe it up, and a tank older than ten years with new noises are the three signs we weigh most heavily. Any one of them means the repair conversation should include a replacement quote.

A leak from the steel shell cannot be patched. Corrosion has opened the tank wall, and the next failure is usually a larger one. Replacing it before it lets go saves the floor, the drywall and often an insurance deductible.

Emergency replacement: what happens after you call

If the tank is leaking now, tell dispatch. We schedule the earliest slot we can, including nights and weekends, and bring the common sizes on the truck.

  • We confirm the fuel type, tank size, venting and where the heater sits.
  • We give you the price for the matched unit before any work begins.
  • We drain, disconnect and haul away the old tank.
  • We set the new heater, connect it to code, test for leaks and show you the shutoff valve.

If you smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 first.

Licensing and permits in Prince George's County

WSSC Water says most plumbing in Montgomery and Prince George's counties must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber or gasfitter, and it names furnace and gas water heater replacements as examples. Permit applications go through WSSC's ePermitting system, and the plumber gives you the permit number so you can schedule the inspection.

WSSC also tells homeowners to ask for the plumber's license number and to verify it by calling 301-206-8588. We encourage that with any contractor, and it is a quick call. Inspectors need an adult to let them inside, so plan to be home or send a representative.

Sizing for Greenbelt homes

Greenbelt Homes, Inc. includes block, brick and frame homes with one to three bedrooms, plus larger townhouses built in 1969. A one-bedroom unit and a three-bedroom townhouse do not need the same tank, and oversizing wastes fuel every day.

For tight utility closets in older row houses, the doorway sometimes decides the model. We measure the opening and the venting path before we order, so the unit that arrives is one that actually fits.

Apartment and condo owners should also ask the association about shutoff rules and approved fuel types, because some buildings restrict gas appliances.

Standards and code for a replacement

A replacement unit should carry the ANSI Z21.10.1 listing if it burns gas or the UL 174 listing if it is electric, and it should be installed to the plumbing and fuel gas code WSSC enforces. That code covers the relief valve discharge, venting, combustion air and the gas shutoff.

Heat pump water heaters are covered by their own listings and manufacturer clearances. Ask for the model number and read its installation manual, because that document is part of what an inspector checks.

How to prepare for the replacement visit

  • Clear a path from the front door to the heater and move stored items from around it.
  • Take a photo of the data plate on the old tank. It lists capacity and fuel type.
  • Know where the main water shutoff valve is and test that it closes fully.
  • If the leak damaged anything, photograph it for your insurer before cleanup begins.
  • Plan for water to be off for several hours.
