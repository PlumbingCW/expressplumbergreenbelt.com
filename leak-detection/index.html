Emergency Leak Detection in Greenbelt, MD

A hidden leak rarely announces itself. It shows up as a high water bill, a warm spot on the floor, a musty smell or a wall stain that grows. Our Greenbelt plumbers use listening gear, pressure tests and thermal imaging to pinpoint the leak before anyone opens a wall.

If water is spraying or pooling now, shut the main valve and call our 24/7 line. For a slow, unexplained loss, the meter test below tells you whether the leak is real.

Start with the meter test

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Find the water meter and look at the leak indicator, the small dial or triangle that spins when any water moves. If it keeps turning, water is leaving somewhere.

Note the meter reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again. A change means a leak somewhere between the meter and the fixtures. Closing the main valve at the house and watching the meter tells you whether the leak is inside the house or on the service line outside.

Active leak: emergency steps

If water is flowing into a room, treat it as an emergency and call the 24/7 line.

  • Close the main water shutoff, usually near the meter or where the line enters the home.
  • Turn off the water heater to protect the element or burner.
  • Move valuables and open the drain line or low spot to limit damage.
  • Photograph the damage before cleanup and keep the receipts.

If the water is near outlets or the electrical panel, stay clear and call an electrician. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility or 911.

Signs of a hidden leak

A bill that jumps with no change in use.

Even a small constant leak runs thousands of gallons over a billing period.

Warm floor spots.

A hot-line leak under a slab heats the surface above it.

Sound of running water at night.

Water moving through a pipe when nothing is on.

Stains, bubbling paint or musty odor.

Moisture in a wall or ceiling needs a source.

Low pressure at every fixture.

A service line leak can reduce what reaches the house.

Slab, wall and under-house leaks

Many Greenbelt homes are built on slabs or crawl spaces rather than full basements, and Greenbelt Homes, Inc. notes that only a limited number of its homes have basements. That makes slab leaks and crawl space leaks the common hidden cases in the older housing.

For slab leaks, we isolate each branch of piping, pressurize it and track the sound of the leak to the surface. We mark the spot, explain the repair choices and only then discuss opening the floor. Rerouting a line through the attic or walls is sometimes cheaper than breaking concrete.

For wall and ceiling leaks, a thermal camera and moisture meter narrow the search to a few square feet, so the repair opening is small.

Water bills, meters and WSSC Water

WSSC Water bills customers for water and sewer based on meter readings. If your bill has risen and you cannot explain it, contact WSSC about the reading, then run the meter test above.

A leak inside the home is usually the homeowner's responsibility. WSSC publishes guidance on who maintains which section of a service line, so check it before assuming either side owns the repair. Fixing a confirmed leak promptly also limits the extra cost on your next bill.

Guidelines we follow for leak testing

WSSC Water's own guidance for customers is the dye tablet test for toilets, 15 minutes with no flushing, repeated twice a year. We use the same guideline as our first test, then add meter testing and pressure isolation for lines.

Pressure testing of repaired piping follows the plumbing code WSSC enforces, which requires new work to be tested before it is covered. A repair that has not been pressure tested is only a guess.

What to do before we arrive for a suspected leak

The best first steps cost nothing and help our plumber find the leak faster.

Read the meter.Turn off every fixture, read the water meter, wait 15 minutes without using water and read it again. Movement means a leak.
Run the dye test.Drop a dye tablet in each toilet tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. Color in the bowl means a flapper leak.
Shut off power near wet areas.If a ceiling or wall is wet near wiring, turn off that circuit, and do not touch outlets with wet hands.
Photograph and note.Take photographs of stains and note when you first saw them, since insurers ask for dates.
