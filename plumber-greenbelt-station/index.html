Plumber in Greenbelt Station, Greenbelt, MD

Greenbelt Station is the newer community just south of the Greenbelt Metro and MARC station. Its townhomes were built in the 2010s, so the plumbing questions are different from those in the older parts of the city.

Here the first step is often a warranty or an association, not a repair. This page lays out who handles what and when a plumber of your own makes sense.

Where Greenbelt Station sits

Prince George's County planning documents describe Greenbelt Station as an 88-acre site just south of the Greenbelt Metro and MARC station, in the plan's south core area. The county's progress table describes it as a transit-oriented residential community of about 500 completed units, finished in 2015 and 2016.

The City of Greenbelt lists the parts of the community that residents will recognize: Greenbelt Station Townhomes built by Ryan Homes, Executive Townes at Greenbelt Station built by NV Homes, the Verde high-rise on Greenbelt Station Parkway and Franklin Park apartments on Springhill Drive.

The Greenbelt Metro station is the northern end of the Green and Yellow lines, which is why many residents here commute by rail.

A newer home still has plumbing questions

A house built in the 2010s is not free of plumbing trouble. Drains clog, supply stops drip, fixtures wear and water heaters stop working in homes of any age. What differs is the order of calls. Builders usually give a warranty on a new home, and the terms vary, so read your closing documents before you pay anyone.

Townhome associations also set rules about exterior work and about which shared lines belong to the association. If a leak might involve a wall you share with a neighbor, tell your association early and photograph the damage.

Water heaters, tankless units and fixtures

Water heater failures are among the most common reasons homeowners call a plumber, in any neighborhood. If your heater is a tankless unit, the manufacturer's manual sets the maintenance schedule, and descaling is a normal part of owning one. Our tankless water heater page explains what that service involves.

Newer homes often have a mix of fixtures chosen by the builder. If a faucet cartridge or toilet fill valve fails, the model number on the fixture lets a plumber bring the right part, so write it down before you call.

Permits and licensed work

Plumbing permits in Prince George's County run through WSSC Water, and its Permit Services office can be reached at 301-206-4003. WSSC also says gas fitting must be done by a licensed plumber or gasfitter, and it can verify a plumber's license at 301-206-8588.

Check that license number before you agree to any work, and keep the permit number if the job needs one.
