Start with how your household uses hot water

The first question is not brand but demand. How many people shower in the morning, whether a dishwasher and washer run at the same time, and whether a tub fills often all change the capacity you need.

A common mistake is buying by tank size alone. First-hour rating, which says how much hot water the heater delivers in its first hour, predicts real performance better than gallons. We read that number for you on every quote.

Venting, gas and drains: the installs that go wrong

Water heaters fail early when basic installation steps are skipped. A missing expansion tank on a closed system raises pressure each time the heater cycles. A relief valve piped to nowhere dumps scalding water on the floor. A poorly pitched flue pulls exhaust back into the room.

We check all three on every job and explain what we find. If your existing flue or gas line cannot support the new unit, we say so and price the correction instead of hiding it in the install.

Permits and license checks

In Prince George's County, WSSC Water requires licensed plumbers or gasfitters for most plumbing and for all gas work, and applications are filed through its ePermitting portal. Some minor jobs may fall under a short form permit, and the plumber supplies the number you use to book the inspection.

Homeowners may do some plumbing under the Plumbing and Fuel Gas Code, but WSSC warns that gasfitting is off limits for DIY work. A water heater installation connects both water and gas, which is why we recommend leaving it to a licensed professional.

Installing in Greenbelt's different housing types

Space for a water heater depends on the individual home, so measure the utility area before choosing a tank. Greenbelt Homes, Inc. counts ten block homes with basements and twenty-five 1969 townhouses with basements among its housing. Those basements usually give easier access for larger tanks.

In garden apartment buildings, shared chases and shutoff limits can govern where a unit goes. In newer subdivisions around Greenbelt, builders sometimes leave a vent or a drain stub that makes a heat pump tank simpler to place. We look for those shortcuts on the first walk through.

If you are in a cooperative or a condominium, get written approval for the work first.

What the code expects from a new install

WSSC Water's plumbing and fuel gas code sets the standard for relief valve piping, venting, gas shutoffs and expansion control, and inspectors check them. NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, is the reference for gas piping and appliance connections.

A closed system, one with a pressure regulator or backflow device, usually needs an expansion tank so rising pressure does not damage the heater. We follow the manufacturer's installation manual and the code together.
