Tankless Water Heater Filters in Greenbelt, MD: What They Do and When to Clean Them

A tankless water heater has a small mesh filter on the cold water inlet. It catches grit before the grit reaches the heat exchanger, and cleaning it is the cheapest maintenance task on the unit. Most owners should check it at least once a year.

A clogged inlet filter makes the water lukewarm and can trigger an error code. This guide covers how the filter works, how to clean it safely and when a separate scale filter makes sense in Greenbelt.

What the inlet filter actually does

Water enters a tankless heater through a small screen near the cold supply connection. Its job is to stop sand, pipe scale and bits of old gasket from entering the flow sensor and heat exchanger, where they cause errors and wear.

The screen is small, so it fills quickly after plumbing work in the house or a main break on the street. When it clogs, flow drops and the burner may shut off because it sees too little water. The result feels like a heater that cannot keep up.

Signs the filter needs cleaning

Lukewarm water that was fine last month.

Low flow cuts the burner.

Flow error codes.

The unit sees too little water and shuts down.

Weaker flow from hot taps only.

The inlet is restricted before the heater.

Debris in aerators.

Grit is passing the screen or the screen is damaged.

How to clean the inlet filter

Follow the manufacturer's manual for your model. If you are not comfortable with gas appliances, call us.

1

Power and valves.

Turn off the heater, close the cold water valve and the hot water valve, and relieve pressure at a faucet.

2

Remove the filter.

Unscrew the filter cap or housing and pull the screen out. Have a towel and a small bucket ready.

3

Rinse and inspect.

Rinse it under running water and brush gently. Replace it if the mesh is torn.

4

Reassemble and test.

Reinstall, reopen the valves slowly, check for leaks and run hot water for a minute.

Inlet screen versus scale filter

The inlet screen is not a scale filter. It stops solid particles, not dissolved minerals. Scale comes from hardness in the water, and it needs either an annual vinegar or descaler flush or a scale-reducing filter upstream of the heater.

Some homeowners add a scale-inhibiting cartridge. These can slow scale buildup but need regular replacement, and they do not replace the flush. Check that any filter you add does not reduce flow below what the heater requires.

The flush that goes with the filter

Cleaning the inlet screen is one task, and descaling the heat exchanger is another. The flush uses a small pump, two hoses and a descaling solution or white vinegar circulated through the unit for about forty-five minutes to an hour, followed by a rinse.

Isolation valves make the flush easy. If your unit does not have them, we can add them during the visit. They also let us shut off the heater for service without draining your home's plumbing.

Signs scale is already building

A rising temperature difference between the set point and the actual temperature, long warm-up times and an error code about the heat exchanger all point to scale. In bad cases you hear popping or rumbling as water boils on a scaled surface.

If you see white flakes in the filter or at the aerators, scale is coming through the system. Schedule a flush sooner than your usual interval.

Other upkeep that prevents error codes

Keep the area around the heater clear of storage. Check the vent for blockages, birds' nests and ice. Make sure the condensate line on a condensing unit drains freely, and replace the neutralizer cartridge on schedule.

A once-a-year visit that covers the filter, the flush, the vent, the combustion check and the relief valve is the best way to get a long life out of the unit.

When to call instead of doing it yourself

Cleaning an inlet screen is a reasonable task for a handy homeowner. Calling is wise if the unit has an error code you cannot clear, if you smell gas, if water leaks from the unit or if the screen cleaned out and the problem remains.

Do not remove the front cover and work on the burner or gas train. That is licensed work, and a mistake can cause a leak or carbon monoxide. If in doubt, turn off the unit and call us.

Choosing a sediment filter for the whole house

If your home often shows grit in the aerators, a whole-house sediment filter ahead of the heater helps. Choose a filter whose flow rating exceeds your peak demand, so it does not starve the heater. Change the cartridge on schedule, since a clogged filter reduces pressure.

Install a bypass valve so you can change the cartridge without shutting off the house. A pressure gauge before and after the filter shows when it needs service.

A quick yearly checklist

Once a year, clean the inlet screen, flush the heat exchanger, inspect the vent, test the relief valve and check the condensate drain. Write the date on the unit. If you keep to that, most error codes never appear. Book the next visit before you leave the house for the season.

Standards for filters and scale control

Filters that treat drinking water can be certified under NSF/ANSI 42 for taste and odor and NSF/ANSI 53 for health-related contaminants. Check the box for the certification, since a filter that lists no standard has made no verified claim.

Descaling a tankless heater follows the manufacturer's instructions, because the solution, flow rate and time differ by model. WSSC's tap report is the official source for local hardness.

Greenbelt water and your tankless unit

WSSC Water's 2023 tap report lists yearly average hardness of 65 mg/L for water from the Patuxent plant and 140 mg/L for water from the Potomac plant. At 140 mg/L, hardness is roughly 8 grains per gallon, enough to build scale in a tankless heat exchanger within a few years. Ask WSSC at 301-206-4001 which plant supplies your street, then set your flush schedule accordingly.

WSSC also says that most plumbing in Prince George's County must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber. Cleaning a filter is routine maintenance, but gas, venting and new connections are not. Use a licensed plumber for those. Owners in Beltsville and Lanham ask us the same filter questions, and the answer depends on which plant supplies the street.
