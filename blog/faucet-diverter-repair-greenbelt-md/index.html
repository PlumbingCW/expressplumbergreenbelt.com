Faucet Diverter Repair in Greenbelt, MD: Fixing a Tub or Shower That Won't Switch

A diverter sends water from the tub spout to the shower head or from the faucet to a sprayer. When it wears out or clogs with scale, water keeps running from the spout while the shower is on, or the shower head only trickles.

Most diverter repairs are quick. The trick is knowing which type you have, since the fix differs for a spout diverter, a three-handle valve and a pressure-balance valve.

Spout diverters: the most common failure

The little gate inside a tub spout wears or collects scale and stops sealing. Water then dribbles from the spout while the shower is running. If the pull-up knob does not stay up, the diverter is done.

The fix is to replace the spout, which unscrews or releases with a set screw underneath. Check whether your spout is threaded or slip-on before buying a new one. Many leaks around the wall come from a spout installed without sealant.

Why scale kills diverters

A diverter has small passages and a seal that moves. Scale hardens the seal and clogs the passages, which is why hard water shortens their life. If your hot water side is worse than the cold, scale from the water heater may be contributing.

Soak a clogged cartridge in white vinegar for several hours and rinse. If it still sticks or the seals are cracked, replace it. A cartridge that is a few dollars of parts can save a ruined shower wall from a hidden leak.

Kitchen faucet sprayers and diverters

In a kitchen faucet, the diverter is a small valve inside the spout body. When you pull the sprayer or press the button, it sends water to the hose instead of the spout. If the sprayer has weak flow and the spout still runs, the diverter is stuck or full of scale.

Unscrew the aerator and look for debris, remove the spout and clean the diverter with vinegar, or replace it. If the sprayer hose is kinked or the hose connection is leaking under the sink, that is a different repair and the fix is a new hose.

Shower valves that will not stay in shower mode

In a pressure-balance or thermostatic valve, the shower mode and the temperature are both controlled by one cartridge. If the shower pushes water out of the tub spout, the cartridge or the diverter stem is worn. A cartridge costs little, but the right one depends on the brand and year.

Take a photo of the handle and trim, note any brand mark and tell us. We bring the common cartridges to the visit. In an older house with an unknown valve, we may need to open an access panel to see the model.

When to replace the whole valve

If the valve body is leaking, the handles spin without engaging or the shutoffs are corroded, replacing the whole valve is the right move. It means opening the wall behind the shower, so plan for tile or panel repair afterward.

A good time to replace is during a bathroom remodel when the wall is already open. Replace the valve and the shower arm at the same time, and add an access panel so the next repair is easy.

Parts to have ready before the visit

Photos help. Take a picture of the handles, the spout and the trim plate, and note any brand name stamped on them. Look for a model number on the back of the handle or on the box if you kept it. Note whether the spout has a set screw underneath or threads on.

If you plan to replace the faucet during the repair, choose the finish and style in advance. We can bring the correct valve trim kit so the new trim matches the valve behind the wall.

A short guide to testing the repair

After the new part is in, test every setting. Run the tub spout, then switch to the shower and check that no water drips from the spout. Switch back and check that the shower head stops. Run hot and cold alone, then mixed, and watch for leaks at the handle and the escutcheon.

Leave the water on for ten minutes and look at the ceiling below for any sign of dampness. A small leak shows up there before it shows anywhere else.

Product standards behind diverters and faucets

Faucets and their internal parts are made to ASME A112.18.1, and parts that touch drinking water should carry NSF/ANSI 61 marks. Those standards are a reason to buy a certified replacement cartridge and to avoid unmarked parts.

Anti-scald performance for shower valves is covered by ASSE 1016 and ASME A112.18.1. If a shower swings hot or cold while you adjust it, the valve is a safety issue and not only a nuisance.

What to do before you call about a shower that swings hot or cold

A valve that changes temperature suddenly can scald, so these safety steps come first.

Lower the water heater setting.Set the heater to a safer temperature if you can reach it, since a lower setting limits scald risk while the valve is faulty.
Test with your hand.Run the water and check it with your hand before anyone steps in. Keep young children and older adults out of the shower.
Do not force the handle.Forcing a stuck handle can crack the valve body and turn a small repair into a wall repair.
Find the shutoff.Know where the bathroom shutoff or main valve is so you can stop water if the valve fails.

Hard water and licensing around Greenbelt

WSSC Water's 2023 tap report lists yearly average hardness of 65 mg/L for water from the Patuxent plant and 140 mg/L for water from the Potomac plant. The Potomac figure works out to roughly 8 grains per gallon, which is enough to build scale on diverters and cartridges over a few years.

WSSC says most plumbing in Prince George's County must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber and suggests verifying the license by calling 301-206-8588. WSSC's CAP Leak Repair Program, run with Habitat for Humanity Metro Maryland, repairs on-property residential plumbing leaks for eligible customers, so ask WSSC if cost is a barrier. Tub and shower diverter repairs are a common visit in Hyattsville and Berwyn Heights, where older bathrooms still have three-handle valves.
