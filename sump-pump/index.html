Sump Pump Repair and Installation in Greenbelt, MD

A sump pump keeps groundwater from flooding a basement or crawl space. When it fails during a storm, the water rises quickly. We repair and replace pumps and install battery backups so the pump runs through a power outage.

Sump pump failure during a storm

If the pump has stopped and water is rising, work quickly.

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped.
  • Lift the float by hand. If the pump runs, the float is stuck.
  • If the pump is dead, bail water or use a wet vacuum to slow the rise.
  • Move belongings off the floor and call the 24/7 line.

Do not stand in water if electrical outlets or the panel are below the waterline. Call an electrician or leave the area.

Signs your sump pump is failing

It runs constantly.

A stuck float or undersized pump never catches up.

It never runs even in heavy rain.

A dead switch or a pit that is not collecting water.

Loud grinding or rattling.

Worn bearings or debris in the impeller.

It is over seven years old.

Pumps wear out, and a storm is the wrong time to find out.

Water pools but doesn't drain.

A clogged discharge or a failed check valve.

Where the water goes matters

A sump pump discharge must carry water well away from the foundation. If it empties near the house, the same water cycles back into the pit. Discharge lines must also not be connected to the sanitary sewer, which is prohibited because it overloads the sewer system.

Check local rules before you decide where the pipe will end. The City of Greenbelt and Prince George's County have drainage and stormwater requirements, and neighbors' property lines matter too. We can recommend a route, but you should confirm placement with the city or your cooperative.

Greenbelt basements and crawl spaces

Greenbelt Homes, Inc. notes that few of its older homes have basements, and it counts ten block homes and twenty-five 1969 townhouses with basements. Those homes, and the newer subdivisions with finished basements, are the ones where sump pumps matter most.

Many Greenbelt properties sit near streams and parkland in the Anacostia watershed, and heavy rain can raise groundwater quickly. A pump with a backup protects a finished basement from power failures that often come with severe storms.

In crawl spaces, a small pump and a vapor barrier keep moisture from rotting framing and encouraging mold.

Maintaining a pump so it works when it matters

Clean the pit once a year. Silt and debris can jam the float, and a stuck float is the most common failure we see. Pull the pump if it is accessible, rinse the intake screen and check that the float moves freely.

Test the check valve. It stops water from flowing back into the pit after the pump shuts off. A failed valve makes the pump cycle constantly and shortens its life. A rattle when the pump stops is a sign the valve is working. Silence with a pump that cycles quickly can mean it is not.

If you are away for long periods, ask someone to check the pit during storm season, or add a water alarm. For a basement with finished space, a backup pump is cheap compared with replacing flooring and furniture.

Standards and rules for sump systems

Prince George's County's Residential Drainage Manual is the local guideline for sump discharge. It says the outfall should be at least 8 to 10 feet from the foundation wall, and at least 10 feet from the property line on private property.

Electrical connections for pumps follow the National Electrical Code, which calls for ground-fault protection near water. WSSC's rules also keep storm water and groundwater out of the sanitary sewer.
