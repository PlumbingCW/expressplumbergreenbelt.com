Grease and drainage in food service

Restaurants produce a steady stream of fat, oil and food solids. Traps and interceptors catch most of it, but they have to be cleaned on schedule. When they are neglected, grease carries into the lateral and builds up.

We clean lines with a jetter and advise on trap service intervals. A line that is jetted before it is blocked costs less than a Friday night backup that closes the kitchen.

Working with property managers

In apartment buildings, a clog in a shared stack affects units far from the cause. We identify the stack, find the clog and isolate the work so only the affected units lose water.

We send a written summary of each visit to the manager, with photographs, so tenant disputes about responsibility can be settled with facts. Greenbelt has many multi-unit properties and cooperative blocks, and each has its own approval rules that we check before opening a wall.

Permits, backflow and compliance

Commercial plumbing in Prince George's County is regulated through WSSC Water's plumbing program. WSSC Water-licensed plumbers do the work and permits are applied for through its ePermitting system.

Backflow preventers on commercial water services need regular testing, and we can coordinate that with the building's compliance schedule. We tell you what is required instead of selling what is not.

Planned maintenance that prevents emergency calls

Most commercial emergencies have a warning sign weeks earlier. A floor drain that drains slowly, a restroom that gurgles when another fixture flushes and a water heater that takes longer to recover are all signals that a failure is coming. A short walkthrough with a plumber is cheaper than a closed business.

We suggest a seasonal check of shutoff valves, water heater relief valves, floor drains and any exposed pipes in unheated spaces. Tenants and managers should also know where the main shutoff is and how to close it. In a flood, the first five minutes decide how much of the building is affected.

Keep a simple log of calls and repairs. A pattern, such as the same drain backing up every spring, points to a root cause that a camera inspection can confirm. See sewer camera inspection for how we document it.

Water heaters and hot water demand for businesses

A commercial kitchen, a salon or a gym can use a lot of hot water in a short window. A tank that was sized for a smaller business will run cold at the peak. We check recovery rate, not only tank size, because recovery decides whether the last customer of the evening gets hot water.

If a heater fails in the middle of service, we can often set a replacement the same day. Planned replacement is cheaper, and our water heater replacement page explains the choices.

Code and compliance for businesses

Commercial plumbing in Prince George's County is governed by the plumbing and fuel gas code WSSC Water enforces. Food service establishments also fall under WSSC's fats, oils and grease requirements, which set fees and allow fines for violations.

Backflow prevention assemblies are tested to ASSE standards, and the results are reported to the utility. Ask your plumber which assemblies on your property need annual testing.

What to do before we arrive at your business

A few steps protect staff, customers and your inventory while our 24/7 line sends a plumber.

Stop the water.Close the main or the nearest fixture shutoff and tell staff why. Do not let people walk through standing water.
Keep electricity away.If water is near outlets, equipment or a panel, switch off that circuit only if you can reach it dry, and do not touch wet equipment.
Protect stock and records.Move boxes, paper files and electronics off the floor and cover shelving near a ceiling leak.
Document it.Photograph the damage with the time stamp on, since landlords and insurers ask for it.
