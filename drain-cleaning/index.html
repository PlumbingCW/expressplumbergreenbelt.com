When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most slow drains can wait for the next appointment. A drain becomes an emergency when water has nowhere to go, and the signs are usually plain.

If wastewater is rising in a shower while the toilet flushes, or a basement floor drain is bubbling, the blockage is probably in the line that serves the whole house. That is the situation to call about at night or on a weekend.

  • Several fixtures back up at once, such as a tub filling when the washer drains.
  • Sewage smell with water or waste coming up through a floor drain.
  • A toilet that will not flush and a sink that gurgles in the same bathroom.
  • Water standing in a basement near the furnace or water heater.

Do not pour chemical drain openers into a standing blockage. They sit in the pipe, burn skin on contact and make the next plumber's job harder.

Where the clog really is

A single slow sink is a branch problem. Kitchen lines collect grease and food, bathroom lines collect hair and soap, and both are usually reachable from the fixture or a nearby cleanout with a hand auger or an electric cable machine.

When two or more fixtures slow down together, the blockage is further down, in the main drain that carries everything toward the sewer. That line runs under the floor or slab, so the cable has to enter through a cleanout, a pulled toilet or a roof vent.

We ask three questions on the phone: which fixtures are affected, whether the toilet is one of them, and whether water appears anywhere it should not. Those answers decide which tool goes on the truck.

How we clear a drain

1

Find the cleanout.

We locate the nearest access point so the cable travels the shortest path to the clog.

2

Cable or snake.

A cable with the right head cuts through grease, hair and soft roots without scarring the pipe.

3

Run water and test.

We flush every fixture on the line and watch the flow, not only the one that failed.

4

Camera if it returns.

If the same line has clogged before, a camera shows whether a belly, crack or root mass is the cause.

Greenbelt homes and the clogs they get

Old Greenbelt was built in 1937 and its original homes are concrete block and brick row houses grouped around shared courts. Greenbelt Homes, Inc. reports roughly 1,600 homes in the cooperative, and its own materials say it covers maintenance and repair of plumbing for members, so call the co-op first if you live in one of its homes. For a repeat clog in any home, a camera look shows the real cause instead of a third round of snaking.

If you live in a cooperative home, check with the cooperative's maintenance office before you book. Some repairs may fall to the co-op, and knowing that first can save a service call.

In the garden apartment communities, such as Springhill Lake, a clog that reaches several units is usually a building stack problem. Renters should report the problem to management first, and property managers can call to talk through stack and floor drain clearing.

What to do before we arrive

These steps keep a bad clog from becoming a flood.

  • Stop using water in the house, including the washer, dishwasher and showers.
  • Move rugs, cardboard and stored items away from floor drains and tub edges.
  • Locate the cleanout cap, usually a round plug near the foundation or in the basement, and clear space around it.
  • Note which fixtures are affected. That detail speeds up the diagnosis more than anything else.
  • Keep children and pets away from standing water that may contain sewage.

Codes behind a clean drain line

Drain, waste and vent work in Prince George's County falls under the plumbing code WSSC Water enforces. That standard requires traps, vents and cleanouts in set places, which is why a clog that returns often has a venting or trap cause as well as a blockage.

WSSC's guidance for kitchen grease, which it calls Can the Grease, is the local standard for homeowners: pour grease into a can, cool it and put it in the trash. We follow the same guideline when we advise commercial kitchens.

Hard water, grease and the clog that returns

WSSC Water, which supplies this part of Prince George's County, reported yearly average hardness of 65 mg/L from its Patuxent plant and 140 mg/L from its Potomac plant in its 2023 tap water report. Treated water from the Potomac side is on the harder end, and harder water leaves more mineral scale on pipe walls over the years. That scale narrows a drain and gives grease and hair something to catch on.

WSSC says the middle section of Prince George's County receives blended water, so ask WSSC which supply serves your street. If a line clogs again within months, scale or a bellied section is the likelier cause than bad luck.
