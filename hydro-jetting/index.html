Hydro Jetting Services in Greenbelt, MD

Hydro jetting uses a hose and a nozzle to scour the inside of a pipe with high-pressure water. It removes the grease, scale and root hairs that a cable only pokes a hole through, and it leaves the pipe wall clean instead of narrowed.

We use it on main drains that keep backing up, on kitchen lines coated in years of grease and on commercial lines that clog on a schedule. If a single sink is slow, start with drain cleaning instead.

Why a cable is sometimes not enough

A drain cable spins through the middle of a pipe and tears an opening in the blockage. The grease and sludge on the pipe walls stays behind, so the opening closes again and the clog returns.

Jetting works differently. Water leaves the nozzle at high pressure in several directions and scrapes the walls all the way around. When the line is clean the water flows freely, which is why jetting is the usual answer for repeat clogs.

Lines that usually need jetting

Kitchen lines that clog every few months.

Cooled fat clings to the pipe wall and builds in layers.

Main drains with slow flow in every fixture.

Scale and sludge narrow the whole line.

Tree root hairs in a camera image.

Fine roots collect paper and waste at every joint.

Restaurant, church or school lines.

Heavy daily use gives grease and solids little chance to clear on their own.

When we will not jet a line

High-pressure water is wrong for a broken, offset or collapsed pipe. It can make a crack worse or wash soil out through the break. That is why we run a camera first and why we tell you plainly if the line needs sewer line repair instead.

Pipe material and condition matter. If a camera shows a fragile or older line, we reduce pressure and choose a gentler nozzle. If the camera finds a belly, a section that holds standing water, jetting helps for now but it will not fix the low spot.

Why Greenbelt lines clog

Greenbelt Homes, Inc. lists homes built from 1936 to 1941 as block and frame construction, and later 1940s homes as brick and block. The age of a house alone does not tell us what its sewer lateral is made of, so a camera look shows what is really there before anyone recommends a repair.

Mature trees line much of the older part of town, and roots are drawn to the moisture at pipe joints. Hard water also leaves mineral buildup, since WSSC Water reports hardness for both of its plants and the Potomac supply is the harder one.

Restaurants in the shopping areas and apartment buildings face the opposite problem, which is steady grease and wipes. Wipes labelled flushable do not break down like toilet paper, and they bind with grease into ropes that jetting can wash out.

Standards that apply to jetting

Sewer rehabilitation work is described by published standards such as ASTM F1216 for cured-in-place lining, and sewer defects are commonly coded using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program. Jetting does not need a certificate, but the camera work before and after is easier to compare when defects are described in those standard terms.

Local rules matter too. WSSC's guidance says only a registered WSSC Master Plumber may excavate or repair underground sewer pipes, which is the reason we move from jetting to repair only when the footage shows a break.
