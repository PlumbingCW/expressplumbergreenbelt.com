Tankless Water Heater Service in Greenbelt, MD

A tankless heater heats water as it flows, so it never runs out when the heat exchanger is clean and the gas supply is right. Our Greenbelt plumbers install, repair and descale tankless units from the common manufacturers.

Most tankless service calls are not failures. They are scale, a blocked filter or a gas supply that cannot keep up, and a flush often restores the performance you paid for.

Why flushing matters in Prince George's County

A tankless heater has narrow passages in its heat exchanger. Dissolved minerals in the water leave scale on those surfaces as the water is heated, and scale insulates the metal and cuts flow.

WSSC Water's 2023 tap report lists yearly average hardness of 65 mg/L for Patuxent water and 140 mg/L for Potomac water. The Potomac figure works out to roughly 8 grains per gallon. At that level, a yearly flush is a reasonable starting schedule, and heavier use may call for more.

If your heater shows a flow or ignition error code, tell us the code and the last flush date. That tells us whether the first step is descaling or something else.

When a tankless unit needs attention

Lukewarm water that fluctuates.

Scale or a clogged inlet filter reduces flow through the heat exchanger.

Error code on the display.

Codes point to ignition, flame, flow, exhaust or temperature faults.

Water takes longer to heat.

A partly blocked filter or a weak gas supply slows ignition.

Popping or kettle-like noises.

Scale hot spots boil water locally inside the exchanger.

Installing a tankless unit in an older home

Most gas tankless heaters need more gas volume than a tank. Older Greenbelt homes may have gas piping sized for a small tank and a range, and the line may need upsizing. Venting is the other constraint, since many units use special stainless or PVC vent pipe that has to run to the outside.

In Old Greenbelt's concrete block homes, drilling for a new vent has to be planned carefully. Check with the cooperative before changing exterior walls.

Because gas work in Prince George's County must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber or gasfitter, we handle the permit and inspection for you.

Standards for tankless units and venting

Gas tankless heaters are listed under ANSI Z21.10.3, and their vent pipe has to match the material and length the manufacturer's listing allows. Mixing vent materials or exceeding the allowed length are common code failures.

Gas piping follows NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, and WSSC Water's code governs the installation in Prince George's County. Descaling follows the manufacturer's instructions, since the cleaning solution and time vary by model.

Before you call about a tankless problem

  • Write down the error code exactly as shown.
  • Check that the gas valve and water valves to the unit are fully open.
  • Check whether the inlet filter is dirty if you know how to reach it.
  • Note when the last flush was done and whether any other gas appliance is acting up.
  • Tell us the manufacturer and model number from the data plate.
