Plumber in Springhill Lake, Greenbelt, MD

Springhill Lake is one of the largest garden apartment communities on the East Coast, and it is home to a large share of Greenbelt's renters. Plumbing problems here follow a pattern. A renter reports, management decides and a plumber does the work.

This page explains that chain in plain terms, so renters, owners and property managers each know their next step.

About Springhill Lake

The Greenbelt Museum records that Springhill Lake was built beginning in 1963 by Community Builders, Inc., and designed by Cohen Haft and Associates. It holds nearly 2,900 low-rise apartment and townhouse units, completed over seven years, and was considered one of the largest garden apartment complexes on the East Coast at the time. The designers studied Greenbelt's walkways and green spaces and tried to repeat them.

A 2011 trade report described the community as 2,877 rental apartments after a purchase by Fieldstone Properties, with a multimillion dollar renovation program. Ownership and unit counts change over time, so confirm current details with the property. The City of Greenbelt runs a recreation center at 6101 Cherrywood Lane, and Springhill Lake Elementary School serves part of the city.

Renters: report in writing, then follow up

Your lease and Maryland landlord-tenant law decide who must repair building plumbing, so the first move is to report a failure to management in writing and keep a copy. Add the date, the unit number and a short description, and attach photos with the time stamp on.

Do not hire your own plumber for building plumbing without asking management, because your lease may forbid it. If the problem is an emergency, such as water pouring from a ceiling, shut off the supply under the fixture if you can reach it, then call management's emergency number.

For property managers and owners

A clog that shows up in several units may point to a shared stack instead of a single fixture. A camera look and a cleaning of the line from a cleanout can show the cause, as our sewer camera inspection page explains.

When you hire any plumber for building work, ask for the license number, check it with WSSC at 301-206-8588 and ask whether a permit is needed. Keep the written scope of work with your building records.
