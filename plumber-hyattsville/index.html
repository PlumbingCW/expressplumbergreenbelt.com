What Hyattsville calls us about, in order

Hyattsville was incorporated in 1886 and had 21,187 residents in 2020 across 8,673 housing units. Older housing drives the demand. The most common calls are aging supply pipes, sewer laterals that have failed, water heaters, and basement water after storms.

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock across the whole city. The details below show how we handle each of the top calls.

Victorian houses and Sears bungalows

The Hyattsville Historic District, listed on the National Register in 1982 and extended in 2004, includes Victorian houses from the late 1880s and Sears bungalows and Arts and Crafts houses from the 1910s to the early 1940s. Houses with that history usually have original cast iron stacks and galvanized supply lines somewhere in the walls.

Historic houses call for careful work. We open as little wall as we can, take photographs before and after and avoid damaging original trim. If the house is in the historic district, check whether exterior changes need approval before we start.

Rowhouses, apartments and shared stacks

Hyattsville has many attached houses and apartment buildings, and a shared drain stack means a backup in one unit can come from another. We start by asking which units are affected and where the nearest cleanout is, because that tells us where the clog sits.

With a manager's permission we clear the stack from the roof vent or the lowest cleanout and run a camera to confirm it is open. For tenants, a written report helps the landlord decide whether the stack needs a repair or only a cleaning.

Hot water in a rowhouse often comes from a tank in a closet barely larger than the heater. Measure the door before ordering, because a tankless unit or a slimmer tank can be the only option that fits.

Storms, basements and the Anacostia watershed

Hyattsville drains toward branches of the Anacostia River, and heavy rain moves a lot of water through its older streets. Bungalow basements and low yards take it first. A sump pump with a battery backup is the cheapest protection for a finished basement.

If water enters through the wall rather than the floor, the cause is often a gutter or grading problem. We look at the outside first, because fixing a downspout costs far less than a drain system.

Codes that apply to Hyattsville work

Hyattsville is served by WSSC Water. Its plumbing code, permit and inspection process applies, and gas fitting follows the NFPA 54 standard, the National Fuel Gas Code. Older houses in the historic district may also have exterior rules, so ask the City before changing anything visible.

WSSC's guidance says only a registered Master Plumber may repair underground sewer pipes. Verify any contractor's license at 301-206-8588.

Arts district shops, Metro stops and Landover Hills

The Gateway Arts District, the area around Hyattsville Crossing and The Mall at Prince George's and the streets near West Hyattsville Metro all have businesses that depend on working restrooms and kitchens. Our commercial plumbing crews handle after-hours calls so shops can open on time.

Hyattsville is served by five ZIP codes and uses 20782 as its main one. Landover Hills, next door, is covered from the same dispatch. WSSC Water supplies the area, and a WSSC Water-licensed plumber handles permitted work.
