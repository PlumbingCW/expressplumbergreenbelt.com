Commercial Plumber in Roosevelt Center, Greenbelt, MD

Roosevelt Center is the original shopping center of Greenbelt, a walkable cluster of shops, restaurants and services at the heart of Old Greenbelt. It is a commercial district, not a neighborhood of homes, and its plumbing needs are those of small businesses.

This page is for owners and managers who run a restaurant, shop or service business in the center and want to know what to check and who to call.

About Roosevelt Center

The Greenbelt Museum explains that all of Greenbelt's businesses were originally in a shopping center at the middle of town, within easy walking distance of the homes. That center is now called Roosevelt Center. It still includes the original movie theater, a cooperative grocery store, several restaurants and a day spa.

Residents once bought shares in a cooperative that ran the grocery, gas station, variety store, movie theater and other shops, with early funding from Boston businessman Edward Filene. Many of those uses have changed, but the walkable layout remains.

Because it is a commercial district, the useful plumbing questions are about kitchens, restrooms, floor drains and the lines that serve them, not about household fixtures.

Grease and kitchen drains

Restaurants and cafes in the center share a concern with food businesses everywhere, which is grease. WSSC Water runs a fats, oils and grease program for food service businesses, with fees and penalties for violations, and its Can the Grease message for homes and businesses is the same: collect grease, let it cool and put it in the trash.

If a kitchen floor drain backs up more than once, a camera look shows whether the problem is grease, solids or a damaged section. Our hydro jetting page explains how a line is cleaned, and drain cleaning covers lighter jobs.

Owner or tenant: who fixes what

In a leased space the lease usually says who handles drains, water heaters and fixtures. Read it before you call, and tell the building owner about any problem that may involve shared lines. The building and its pipes may have been changed many times over the years, so a camera inspection is a better guide than a guess.

For work beyond a simple repair, ask your plumber for a written scope and the license number, and verify the license with WSSC at 301-206-8588. Ask whether the job needs a WSSC permit, which the plumber normally arranges.
