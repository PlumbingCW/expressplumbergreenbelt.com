Check any plumber's license before you hire

WSSC Water says most plumbing in Prince George's County must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber or gasfitter, including work such as furnace and gas water heater replacement. Ask for the license number and verify it by calling WSSC at 301-206-8588.

Permits go through WSSC's ePermitting system, and the plumber gives you the permit number so you can book the inspection. We handle that for the work we do, and we encourage the same check for any contractor.

Greenbelt's planned-town housing and your plumbing

Old Greenbelt was laid out in the 1930s as a planned greenbelt town and is a National Historic Landmark district. Its block, brick and frame homes were built from the late 1930s onward. In any house type, a hidden leak shows up first as a damp floor, a stain or a rising bill, so we start with meter and listening tests before opening anything.

Springhill Lake is a large garden apartment community, where a clog in a shared stack can flood units far from the cause. Property managers can call to talk through isolating the stack. Around Roosevelt Center and the shops on Greenbelt Road and at Beltway Plaza, restaurants and stores rely on drains that handle grease all day, so scheduled jetting costs less than a closed kitchen.

Greenbelt Lake and the parkland along the city sit near the water table, and heavy rain can push groundwater into low crawl spaces. If you smell damp or see moisture there, it deserves an early look. WSSC Water supplies the area and issues plumbing permits, so permitted work is done by a licensed plumber.
