Old Town, Calvert Hills and the campus edge

Old Town College Park was platted in 1889 and sits between the University of Maryland, the B&O tracks and US Route 1. The National Register district, listed in December 2012, covers about 120 acres of American Foursquare and Cape Cod houses, plus garden-style apartments nearer campus.

A neighborhood built out over several decades has plumbing from several eras, often inside the same house. Original galvanized lines meet newer copper and plastic at odd places, and those junctions are where leaks tend to appear. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for all of it.

Fraternity and sorority houses and large shared rentals add a second load. A house with eight residents uses water the way a small business does, so drains and water heaters wear faster than their ratings suggest.

Renters, landlords and who calls whom

In Maryland the landlord is generally responsible for keeping plumbing in working order, so a tenant should report a failure in writing as soon as it happens. We can send a written summary of our visit with photographs, which landlords and property managers use for insurance and repairs.

Owners of rental houses near campus often ask us for a yearly check of water heaters, shutoffs and main drains. A tank that is already ten years old going into a fall semester is a risk worth pricing before it fails. Our water heater repair page explains what we look at.

Move-in season and move-out season

Rental plumbing in College Park peaks twice a year. At move-in, new tenants find problems that sat unused all summer, such as seized valves and tanks that never recovered. At move-out, a full house of use ends with drains packed with everything a household flushes.

Landlords who schedule a pre-season check of water heaters, shutoff valves and main drains spend less than those who wait for a midnight call. Ask us about a seasonal visit.

Codes that apply to College Park work

Plumbing in College Park falls under the code WSSC Water enforces, with permits through WSSC and, for building work, the County. WSSC also runs the FOG program, with fees and fines for food service sites that let grease reach the sewer.

Gas fitting must be done by a licensed plumber or gasfitter, and NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, is the reference standard. Rental owners can verify a plumber's license with WSSC at 301-206-8588.

Water, permits and Route 1

WSSC Water supplies College Park and issues plumbing permits across Prince George's County. WSSC says most plumbing here must be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber and advises homeowners to check the license number by calling 301-206-8588. We encourage that call with any contractor.

Route 1 shops and restaurants face a different problem than houses do. Sewer laterals under busy strips carry grease and cooking waste, and a camera look at the line is the fastest way to see why it keeps backing up. See sewer line repair if the camera shows damage.

Riverdale Park, just south, is covered from the same dispatch base, so a plumber heading to College Park can usually reach it as well.
