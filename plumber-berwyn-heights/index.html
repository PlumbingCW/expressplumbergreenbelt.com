Winter: frozen pipes and bursts

Berwyn Heights began in 1888 as Charlton Heights, a railroad suburb, and its first houses went up between 1889 and 1892, many from Victorian pattern-book plans. Houses from that era often have pipes in exterior walls and unheated crawl spaces, which are where a hard freeze does its damage.

If a pipe has frozen, leave the faucet slightly open, warm the area gently and call. A frozen pipe often cracks and only leaks once it thaws, so the water arrives later than the cold. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock on the coldest nights.

Spring: rain, roots and drains

Spring rain pushes groundwater up, and drains that are already tight start to back up. Houses built after the town's post-war building boom, from 316 homes in 1955 to about 1,000 by 1970, often have cast iron or clay laterals that roots have found by now.

If one fixture drains slowly, drain cleaning is usually enough. If several do, the main line is the problem and a camera look tells us why.

Summer: water heaters under load

A water heater that has run for fifteen years often fails in the first cold snap of the next winter, but the warning signs show in summer. Lukewarm showers, popping noises and rust-tinted hot water all say the tank is finishing.

Replacing a tank on a plan costs less than replacing one on an emergency night. See water heater replacement for what we recommend.

What a small town means for response

Berwyn Heights has roughly 3,000 residents on a handful of grid streets, and the town sits against Greenbelt. That compact layout keeps our travel short, and it means a crew can often reach a street with a call in the morning and finish by midday.

Most calls come from detached houses with a basement or crawl space and a single water heater. We bring the common parts for those, including relief valves, shutoff valves, supply lines and fittings for copper and plastic. When a part has to be ordered, we say so on the phone.

If you are not sure whether your problem is urgent, describe it to dispatch. A slow drip under a sink can wait for a booked visit, but a spreading stain on a ceiling cannot.

Older houses, newer repairs

Many Berwyn Heights houses have been remodeled more than once. A 1950s kitchen with a 1990s bath and a new water heater is a common mix, and each part of the plumbing has its own age. We label what we find on the invoice so the next plumber, or the next owner, sees the history.

When we find galvanized pipe, we tell you how much is left and whether a section replacement is enough. For a whole house with failing lines, repiping is the lasting fix, and we quote it in sections so it does not have to happen all at once.

Codes that apply to Berwyn Heights work

Berwyn Heights is in WSSC territory. The plumbing code WSSC enforces covers permits and inspections, and NFPA 54, the National Fuel Gas Code, is the standard for gas piping and appliances.

WSSC's guidance on service lines explains who owns each section of pipe. Ask for that explanation before agreeing to any work outside your property line.

Town services and Greenbelt Road

Berwyn Heights is an incorporated town with its own commissioners. The town hall opened in 1956 at Berwyn Road and 57th Avenue, and shopping centers line Greenbelt Road along its north edge. Businesses there can reach us on the same 24/7 line as homes.

The town shares the 20740 ZIP code with neighboring College Park. WSSC Water serves the area and issues plumbing permits.
