Sewage backup: what to do right now

A backup that reaches several fixtures at once points to the main line. Take these steps while we are on the way.

  • Stop using water, including toilets, showers, washers and dishwashers.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone vulnerable away from the affected area.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into a backed-up line.
  • Photograph the backup for insurance, then call our 24/7 line.

If sewage is touching electrical outlets or the furnace, leave the area and call an electrician. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility or 911.

Signs the sewer line is damaged

Backups in the lowest drain.

Floor drains and basement showers show a main line problem first.

Gurgling in several fixtures.

Air is being pushed through a partly blocked line.

Sewage smell in the yard.

A break leaks gas and wastewater into the soil.

A wet, sunken or unusually green patch.

Leaking wastewater feeds grass and softens soil.

Repeat clogs after cabling.

A belly, root intrusion or offset joint keeps catching waste.

Who owns which part of the sewer line

WSSC Water says the lateral, the pipe from the property line to the building, is maintained by the property owner. WSSC maintains the public main and, where a service connects to a main in a right-of-way on private property, only the connection to the main.

If a blockage sits in the WSSC-maintained section, WSSC clears it at no charge, and a plumber who cannot clear a line must report it to WSSC's 24-hour line at 301-206-4002. A good plumber will tell you when the problem is not yours to pay for instead of quoting the work anyway.

What older Greenbelt lines tend to look like

Homes in Old Greenbelt were built from the late 1930s onward, but the age of a house does not tell you what its lateral is made of. Clay, cast iron and plastic lines each fail in their own way, so a camera look comes before any repair plan.

Newer subdivisions around Greenbelt usually have PVC laterals, which fail more often through settlement or a poorly made joint than through age. The pipe material tells us which failure to look for first.

Because many older homes sit on slabs, a failed lateral under the floor is a different job from one in the yard, and we plan the access before quoting.

Permits and licensing

In Prince George's County, WSSC Water requires most sewer and drain work to be done by a WSSC Water-licensed plumber, and plumbing permits for this work are applied for through WSSC's ePermitting system.

WSSC advises asking for a plumber's license number and verifying it by phone. Please do that with us as well. A permit also brings an inspection of the finished repair, which protects the next owner as well as you.

Rules and standards for sewer repair

Underground sewer repair in WSSC territory needs a WSSC permit and inspection, and only a registered WSSC Master Plumber may excavate or repair these pipes. Lining uses ASTM F1216 as its reference standard, and replacement pipe is installed to the plumbing code.

Plastic sewer pipe is generally bedded and backfilled following ASTM D2321. Ask for the permit number and the inspection date, and keep both with your records.
