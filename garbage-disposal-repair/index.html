Safety first: never reach into a disposal

Even with the switch off, a disposal can cause serious injury. These steps keep you safe.

  • Turn the switch off, then unplug the unit or switch off the breaker.
  • Never put your hand into the chamber.
  • Use tongs or pliers to remove an object you can see.
  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit after it cools.

If the unit is leaking onto an electrical connection, switch off the breaker and call us.

What your disposal should and should not handle

Disposals handle soft scraps, but they are not trash cans. Grease, coffee grounds in bulk, fibrous vegetables like celery and starchy foods such as rice and pasta can jam the unit or collect in the pipe below.

Grease that goes down the sink cools and sticks to the drain wall. Over months it narrows the line, and a kitchen line that clogs repeatedly may need hydro jetting rather than another cable run.

Disposals in Greenbelt kitchens

Many of Greenbelt's older apartments and cooperative homes have small kitchens with limited cabinet space, so the disposal fits tightly under the sink. A compact model with a removable mounting ring makes service easier.

Some older buildings have rules about disposals because of their drain lines. Check with your landlord or cooperative before installing one.

When we install a disposal, we also check the P-trap and the drain connection, because a poorly installed trap is a common cause of the next clog.

Choosing a replacement disposal

Disposals are sold by horsepower, and more is not always better. A third horsepower unit handles light use. A half to three quarter horsepower unit suits a family kitchen. Higher power units grind finer and run quieter, which matters in apartments with thin walls.

Look at the grind stage. A single-stage unit is cheaper but jams more easily. Multi-stage models with a stainless steel chamber last longer. Sound insulation, a batch feed option and a corrosion-resistant flywheel are worth considering if you will keep the unit for a decade.

Check the mount. Quick-lock mounts let us remove the unit without disturbing the sink flange, which saves time at the next service.

Keeping a disposal and its drain healthy

Run cold water before, during and for about fifteen seconds after use so the waste is flushed down the line. Cold water keeps fats solid so they are chopped, instead of melting and coating the pipe.

Grind a handful of ice or citrus peel occasionally to scrub the chamber. Avoid bones, fruit pits, potato peels in bulk and anything fibrous. If the sink still drains slowly after the disposal works, the clog is in the line below it, and our drain cleaning crews can clear it.

Standards for disposals and safe repair

Household waste disposers are covered by UL 430, which includes tests for the grinding chamber and for protection against shock. The unit's label should show a recognized listing.

WSSC Water's guideline for grease still applies when you own a disposal: can it, cool it and toss it. A disposal grinds food, but it does not make grease safe for the drain.

What to do before we arrive for a disposal problem

A jammed or leaking disposal is easy to make worse. These safety steps keep your hands and your kitchen safe.

Switch off the power.Turn the disposal's wall switch off, then unplug it or shut off its breaker before you check anything.
Keep hands out.Never reach into the grinding chamber. Use tongs or pliers to remove a visible object after the power is off.
Do not run water on a leak.If the unit is leaking at the bottom, stop using the sink and place a bucket underneath.
Skip drain chemicals.Chemical drain cleaners sit in a jammed chamber and can splash. Tell the plumber if you used one.
