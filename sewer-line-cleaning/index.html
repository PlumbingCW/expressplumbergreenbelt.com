Main line backup: first steps

A main line clog affects the whole house. Keep water use to a minimum until we arrive.

  • Stop running water in the house.
  • Locate your cleanout, usually a capped pipe outside or in the basement or crawl space.
  • Do not remove the cap if wastewater is under pressure.
  • Call our 24/7 line and tell us which drains are backing up.

Floodwater from a sewer backup can carry bacteria. Wear boots and gloves, keep children out and do not touch electrical equipment standing in water.

Cleanouts: the cheapest upgrade you can make

A cleanout is a capped pipe that gives machines direct access to the main. Without one, a plumber has to pull a toilet to reach the line, which adds time and risk of damage.

If your home has no exterior cleanout, we can install one when we clear the line. It makes this job and every future one faster, and it lets us run the camera without disturbing any fixtures.

Greenbelt specifics

Greenbelt's older homes sit close to mature trees, and the sewer lateral often runs under a root zone. Roots enter at joints and grow into a mat that catches paper, so root-related backups are a leading cause here.

Apartment buildings and cooperative blocks share lines, so a backup in a lower unit may come from a clog upstream. In shared systems, we check with the property manager or cooperative before pulling a cleanout cap.

WSSC Water maintains the public sewer main. If our camera shows the blockage is in the main rather than your lateral, we stop and tell you to call WSSC instead of billing you for their pipe.

Choosing between a machine and a jetter

A sectional machine is the tool for a hard blockage such as roots or a foreign object. Its cutters break the mass apart. A jetter is the tool for a soft one such as grease and scale. Many calls need both, one to open the line and the other to wash it.

We carry both on the truck, so we choose based on what the line needs and not on what is easiest. If a camera shows a fragile pipe, we use the gentler option and tell you why.

Guidelines behind a proper cleaning

WSSC Water's guidance for a backup says the plumber should snake the line to the WSSC main, and call 301-206-4002 right away if they believe the blockage was cleared in the WSSC section. That guideline protects you from paying for a public-side problem.

Camera follow-up is the standard way to confirm a line is open. Defects are commonly described using NASSCO's Pipeline Assessment Certification Program terms, which gives a reader of the report a shared vocabulary.
