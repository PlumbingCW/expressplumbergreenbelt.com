Plumber in Old Greenbelt, Greenbelt, MD

Old Greenbelt is the original planned town at the center of the city, built in the 1930s and now a National Historic Landmark. Many of its homes belong to a housing cooperative, so the first question about a plumbing problem here is often who to call.

This page explains that split in plain terms, shows where a private plumber fits, and lists the steps that protect your home while you wait for help.

What Old Greenbelt is

Greenbelt was built between 1935 and 1938 as a federal planned community, and the National Park Service describes the original housing as concrete block homes, each with a garden. About 885 residences were nearly complete in 1937, and roughly 1,000 more frame and row houses were added in 1941 for defense workers.

Planners followed the ridge to give the town a crescent shape, with shared walkways and pedestrian underpasses. The Greenbelt Museum says roughly 44 percent of the original houses are cinder block with flat roofs and about 51 percent are brick with gabled roofs.

For a homeowner or renter, the useful point is that houses here come in several types and ages, so two neighbors can have very different plumbing. A technician has to look at your house instead of assuming.

Who to call first: the co-op or a private plumber

The federal government sold the town in 1952, and residents formed Greenbelt Homes, Inc., known as GHI. GHI says it is a housing cooperative of about 1,600 homes and that it covers maintenance and repair of plumbing, wiring and structure for its members. If you live in a GHI home, report the problem to the co-op first, because repairs inside the system may be its job and it may need to approve any outside work.

Not every building in the historic area is part of the co-op. The City of Greenbelt lists several apartment buildings on Parkway and Crescent Road under separate management companies. If you own a home outside the co-op, manage a building or run a business, a private plumber is the right call, and you can reach us to talk it through.

Planning work in a historic district

Greenbelt's designation as a National Historic Landmark is why the town looks the way it does. Before you change anything visible from outside, or cut into structural walls, ask the City and, for co-op homes, GHI whether approval is needed. The City of Greenbelt says projects that need a county building permit also need City permits.

Plumbing permits in the county run through WSSC Water, which says gas fitting must be done by a licensed plumber or gasfitter. You can check any plumber's license with WSSC at 301-206-8588 before you hire, and you should do the same with anyone who works on your pipes.

While you wait for help

These steps limit damage in any home type.

Find the shutoff.Close the main valve where the water line enters the house. In a co-op home, ask the co-op where it is if you do not know.
Keep electricity away.If water is reaching outlets or the panel, turn off that circuit only if you can reach the switch while dry.
Photograph the problem.Take dated photos before you clean up. The co-op, your landlord or an insurer may ask for them.
Tell the right party.Report the problem to the co-op or management in writing, and keep a copy.
