Faucet Repair and Replacement in Greenbelt, MD

A dripping faucet wastes water day and night, and it is rarely a costly fix. Most drips come from a worn cartridge, washer or seat, and we repair or replace them in a single visit.

If the faucet is old, corroded or a model that is no longer made, replacement is often cheaper than hunting for parts.

What a drip usually means

In a compression faucet, the rubber washer wears against the seat. In a cartridge or ball faucet, the cartridge or O-rings wear out. The seat itself can also corrode, which leaves a rough surface that cuts new washers quickly.

A faucet dripping once per second wastes thousands of gallons a year. Because WSSC Water bills water and sewer by usage, a drip you ignore shows up in two parts of your bill.

Outdoor faucets and winter in Prince George's County

Hose bibs can freeze in a cold snap and split the pipe behind the wall. The break may not show until spring, when the faucet is turned on and water pours into the wall.

Disconnect hoses before winter, shut off the interior valve where one exists and drain the faucet. If yours is not frost-proof, we can replace it with a frost-free model that angles water away from the wall.

Choosing a replacement faucet

Cheap faucets have plastic internals that fail early, while a good one lasts years. Look for solid brass construction, a ceramic disc cartridge and a warranty that covers parts.

Finish matters in hard water. WSSC Water's yearly report lists hardness for both its plants, and the Potomac supply is the harder one. Scale builds on spouts and aerators, so a model with a removable aerator is easier to maintain.

In older Greenbelt kitchens, the shutoff valves under the sink are often original. We replace them when we install the faucet if they are corroded, because a valve that will not close is a risk on the next repair.

Kitchen, bathroom and utility faucets are different jobs

A kitchen faucet works hard. It runs hot water several times a day, it carries a sprayer hose and it sits above a cabinet where a slow leak can hide for months. A bathroom faucet is simpler but its aerator clogs with scale, so low flow is the common complaint. Utility sink faucets and laundry valves see the least use and often fail from corrosion instead of wear.

Because the symptoms overlap, we test the whole fixture. A faucet that drips from the spout, leaks under the handle or sprays sideways each points to a different part. Replacing the wrong part wastes your money, which is why we diagnose before we repair.

When we find a failing supply line, we replace it with a braided line while we are there. A burst supply line is a common cause of cabinet flooding, and it costs little to prevent.

When a faucet leak is a sign of something bigger

A faucet that sputters, hammers or shakes can point to air in the line or a pressure issue elsewhere. If every faucet in the house does the same, the cause is the system and not the fixture. A pressure check at a hose bib tells us whether the water service is the problem.

Discolored water from only the hot side may mean sediment in the water heater. See water heater repair for that. Persistent low pressure at every tap deserves a look at the service line, and leak detection explains how we test for loss before the meter.

Product standards for faucets

Faucets are built to ASME A112.18.1, and the parts that touch drinking water are certified to NSF/ANSI 61 and NSF/ANSI 372 for material safety and low lead content. Those marks are on the box and often on the faucet body.

Following these standards is a reason to buy a faucet with its certifications printed on it and to avoid unmarked imports. Replacement cartridges are specific to each brand, so match them by model number.
