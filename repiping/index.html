Whole House Repiping in Greenbelt, MD

Repiping replaces the supply pipes that bring water to every fixture. It makes sense when leaks keep appearing in different places, when water runs brown or when pressure has faded across the whole house.

We replace the lines in sections, restore the walls and keep water on for as much of the project as we can. We also replace the water service line, the pipe from the meter to the house, when it is part of the problem.

Signs your supply pipes are near the end

Galvanized steel rusts from inside and sheds into the flow.

The water service line: the part people forget

The service line carries water from the main in the street to your house. It is buried, so a leak may only show as a high bill or a soft patch of lawn. Older service lines may be copper or galvanized steel and can corrode or crack.

WSSC Water says it is responsible for the portion that connects to the water main and runs to the property line, and that the customer owns the portion from the property line into the home. Replacing the interior pipes and leaving a corroded customer-side line in place wastes money, so we check the service line while we assess the house and tell you which part falls to you before we quote.

Repiping in Greenbelt's housing

Greenbelt Homes, Inc. describes its older homes as concrete block, brick and frame buildings, many of them without full basements. That affects where pipes can run. In a slab-on-grade or crawl space home, new lines often go through attics, closets and chases to avoid breaking concrete.

In multi-unit buildings and cooperative blocks, repiping may involve shared risers and require approval before work starts. Ask your cooperative or association for its rules first. We coordinate shutoff times with neighbors so no one is surprised by a water outage.

Because WSSC Water-licensed plumbers do this work under permit, the finished system is inspected. Ask for the permit number so you can book the inspection.

Planning for living through a repipe

Most families stay in the house during a repipe. We set up temporary water where practical and tell you in advance when the water will be off. Plan for a day or two of limited use, especially if your kitchen and bath are being done at the same time.

Move furniture and valuables away from the walls where we will cut. We protect floors and use dust control, but drywall work creates dust. Pets and young children are best kept clear of the work area.

After the new lines are in, flush every faucet to clear debris, clean the aerators and check the water heater connections. Pressure and flow should be noticeably better. If they are not, tell us the same day.

Piping standards and what they mean

Cross-linked polyethylene tubing is made to ASTM F876 and F877, and copper water tube to ASTM B88. Both are accepted materials, and the fittings must match the pipe's standard. Mixing fittings from one system with tubing from another is a common cause of leaks.

Drinking-water pipe and fittings should carry NSF/ANSI 61 marks. WSSC Water enforces the plumbing code for the installation and inspects the finished system, and its guidance on service lines explains who owns each section.
